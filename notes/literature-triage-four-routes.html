From a research question to an evidence table: four routes compared

Short answer

Four tools sit at four different points on the same pipeline, which is why comparisons between them usually go in circles.

Undermind is a literature agent: it searches, ranks and writes up what exists. Elicit searches a very large corpus and generates structured reports and extraction tables. SciSummary compresses papers into readable sections and explains the statistics inside them. HumanPen Start Project does none of those — it takes your data or your documents and returns an analysed deliverable with figures, tables, references and slides.

The useful question is not "which is best" but "which step is currently blocking me". This page maps the steps so you can answer it in a minute rather than a week of trials.

Route one: Undermind, for the shape of a field

Undermind's page frames the product as "your AI co-researcher for the literature", claims 2× as many relevant papers as AI web search, and shows a completed search returning 57 relevant papers before producing a writeup in which citations are attached to claims. It names research institutions among its users and states plainly that it fetches open-access PDFs while you upload the rest.

That last sentence is the most honest line on any of these pages. No literature agent has full text to everything; the ones that admit it are easier to plan around.

Choose it when your problem is coverage: you suspect a body of work exists that your keyword searches are missing.

Route two: Elicit, for extraction at scale

Elicit's page cites 138 million academic papers and 545,000 clinical trials, semantic search that does not require the right keywords, and reports built on a systematic-review-inspired process. It also publishes a case in which a policy-facing review's data extraction matched 1,502 of 1,511 data points.

That case is the relevant evidence for anyone building an evidence table: the claim is not "we summarize", it is "we extract into columns at a measurable accuracy". If your review needs forty columns across thousands of papers, this is the family of tool to evaluate.

Route three: SciSummary, for comprehension

SciSummary does something the others do less explicitly: it explains. Papers are broken into clearly labelled sections, and reported statistics are interpreted — p-values, confidence intervals, effect sizes — in language a reader can use. It supports bulk summarization across several papers, and states it was founded in 2023 and is the official AI provider for the ACM Digital Library.

This is the right tool when you are the reader, not the author. Journal club, onboarding into a new field, triaging a reading list before a meeting.

Route four: HumanPen, for the artifact

HumanPen's project page describes a different loop entirely: attach files and describe what you need, the system confirms requirements and proposes a plan, work starts after your approval, and a second AI checks before reports, charts and code are delivered. Intake accepts Excel, CSV, Word, PDF and zip up to 5 GB total; analysis runs on Python or R, and work originally done in SPSS or Stata is performed in Python or R; GPU training is not supported.

The page's worked example is a re-analysis: a six-month death-or-dependency endpoint at 62.2% versus 63.5%, not significant at P = 0.072, with a sensitivity analysis for missing outcomes and a first-round review that passed with all 11 references found one by one.

That is not literature work. It is the step after the literature: your data, a defensible table, and a document.

Where the handoffs break

Three handoffs cause most of the lost weeks.

From search to extraction. A ranked reading list is not a table. Someone must decide the columns, then fill them, then check the fills. Elicit is built for that; Undermind stops one step earlier.

From extraction to analysis. An evidence table of other people's effect sizes is not your result. If the paper needs a pooled estimate or a re-analysis, you have moved into a different category of work.

From analysis to document. This is where HumanPen sits, and where most deadlines actually die: correct numbers, wrong table format, figures at screen resolution, a deck built by screenshot.

A decision rule that survives contact with a deadline

  • If you cannot name the ten papers that matter: Undermind or Elicit.
  • If you can name them but cannot parse the methods: SciSummary.
  • If the table of extracted findings is the deliverable: Elicit.
  • If the table must contain your own computed estimates: HumanPen.
  • If the deadline is a submission and the document does not exist: HumanPen, and start with the plan.

FAQ

Can I replace all four with one? No, and any vendor claiming so is describing its own scope loosely. Search, extraction, comprehension and computation are four different contracts.

Which pair covers the most ground? Elicit plus HumanPen covers discovery-to-delivery for data-driven work; Undermind plus HumanPen covers it for theory-driven work.

Is HumanPen useful before I have data? Marginally — it reads documents you upload. Its value starts when there is something to analyse.
