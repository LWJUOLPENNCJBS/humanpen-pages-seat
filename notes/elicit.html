Elicit vs HumanPen Start Project: Screening 5,000 Papers vs Running Your Own Dataset

Short answer

Choose Elicit when the bottleneck is the literature itself. Its public pages describe semantic search over "over 138 million academic papers and 545,000 clinical trials", a Systematic Review Workflow on the Pro plan that can screen 5,000 papers, and table building where you add 5, 20 or 30 columns at a time depending on the plan. That is a screening and extraction engine, and the company's own case study — VDI/VDE extracting "1,502 out of 1,511 data points" for an accuracy rate of "99.4%" — is the clearest statement of what it is built to do.

Choose HumanPen Start Project when the bottleneck is your own dataset. You attach Excel, CSV, Word, PDF or zip files up to 5 GB total, describe the analysis, confirm a plan, and Python or R runs in an isolated sandbox. What comes back is a .docx report, three-line tables, 300 dpi figures, a .pptx with speaker notes and the code that produced the numbers — after a second AI has reviewed the delivery.

Both tools end up producing a table with citations attached to it. The difference is where the rows come from. Elicit's rows come out of published papers. Start Project's rows come out of a dataset you uploaded.

Where the workflows actually diverge

1. The unit of work: a column, or a plan

Elicit's interface is organised around the table. You define the fields you want, and the product fills them across a set of papers, adding 5 columns at a time on Plus, 20 on Pro, 30 on Scale, 40 on Enterprise. The granularity of the work is one column across many papers.

Start Project's unit is the project. You describe the task and attach files, the system confirms requirements and proposes a plan, and work begins only after you approve it. The page is explicit about the gate: "Plan first, start when you confirm." Five stages follow — Describe, Plan, In progress, In review, Delivered.

Those are different mental models. Elicit rewards a researcher who already knows which fields matter and wants them populated at scale. Start Project rewards one who knows the question but wants the whole execution path handed over: cleaning, model, tables, figures, prose.

2. Citations versus reproducible numbers

Elicit's answer surface is built around its sources: on every plan, including the free one, you can view the sources behind an answer. For a synthesis of other people's findings, that is the property you want.

Start Project's assurance works differently. The published example is a secondary analysis of the International Stroke Trial: a full analysis set of 19,435 patients, a primary endpoint of death or dependency at 6 months, about 150 missing outcomes handled with a sensitivity analysis, aspirin group 62.2% against control 63.5%, OR 0.95 (0.89–1.00), P = 0.072. The page's review panel checks that "a clean rerun reproduces the results exactly" and that all 11 references were found one by one, with PubMed search and every DOI checked.

A citation tells you where a sentence came from. A rerun tells you whether the table is arithmetic. For an evidence table built from papers, you want the first. For a Table 1 built from 19,435 rows, you want the second.

3. What "analysis" means in each product

Elicit's analysis is bibliographic. Its Research Agent sits on the home page as the entry point for high-stakes research, and its reports synthesise published work: on Pro a report extracts from up to 135 data sources, on Scale up to 200. What the product composes is an account of what the literature reports, drawn paper by paper.

Start Project's analysis is statistical: regression, survival analysis, baseline tables, forest plots, subgroup figures. The upload formats it accepts are the give-away — Excel, CSV, Word, PDF, zip — and the FAQ is blunt about the boundaries: Wind or CNKI data must be exported first, and GPU training is not yet supported.

If your task is "what does the literature say about X", Elicit's framing is closer. If your task is "what does my data say about X", Start Project's is.

Uploads and what you actually receive

Elicit's input is a question plus a corpus: Zotero import on the free tier, and figure extraction on Scale, where the product will "extract and interpret from figures in research papers". Outputs leave as RIS, CSV, BIB, PDF and DOCX from Plus upward — the right shape if the next stop is a reference manager or a manuscript.

Start Project's input is up to 5 GB total of Excel, CSV, Word, PDF and zip. Its outputs are a folder: the .docx report, the figures at 300 dpi to journal standards, the three-line tables and captions, a .pptx with speaker notes for a lab meeting or defence, and the code. Revisions are requested inside the project: the scope is explained first, work starts after you confirm, a new version is delivered and past versions are kept.

That last point matters. An extraction table is usually finished in one pass; an analysis chapter is not. A reviewer asks for a subgroup, and you need the old version to still exist.

Pricing and governance

Elicit publishes a five-tier ladder. Free carries limited usage for the Research Agent and Research Reports while search and summaries stay unlimited. Plus is $11 per user/month billed as $132 annually. Pro is $39 per user/month billed as $468 annually and is the first tier with the Systematic Review Workflow and API access. Scale is $89 per user/month billed as $1,068 annually and adds real-time collaboration. Enterprise is custom, screens 40,000 papers, extracts 40 columns, and states "No training on your data by default" alongside SSO & SAML.

The figures above are the annual-billing view; the page also has a monthly toggle.

On governance, the two products answer different questions. Elicit's verifiable data statement sits at the Enterprise tier: no training on your data by default, plus SSO & SAML and enterprise controls. Start Project claims more lower down the ladder — encrypted in transit, every project in its own isolated sandbox, never shared across users, never used for training, deletable completely at any time — and keeps projects for 6 months after the last activity, with a reminder 3 days before expiry.

We found no retention period on Elicit's public pages, so we assert none.

When to choose which

Choose Elicit if:

  • You need to screen thousands of abstracts against inclusion criteria — 5,000 papers on Pro, 40,000 on Enterprise.
  • The deliverable is a synthesis: an evidence table, a structured report, a review of what is already published.
  • You want Routines, which appear from the Pro tier upward.
  • Your team needs shared evidence and live editing, which is the Scale tier's model.

Choose HumanPen Start Project if:

  • You already have the data — a survey file, a cohort extract, trial-level records, a panel — and the missing step is the statistics.
  • The analysis was done in SPSS or Stata and needs to be redone in Python or R with the code kept.
  • The deliverable is a report with three-line tables, 300 dpi figures, a methods-consistent narrative and slides, reviewed before it reaches you.
  • You expect revisions and want version history rather than a fresh chat each time.

Use both if your project has two halves. Screen and extract in Elicit, export the extraction table as CSV, then start a project with that CSV plus your own outcome data and let Python or R do the pooling, the subgroup work and the figures. The first half is a literature problem; the second is a computation problem. Neither product's public pages claim to do the other half well, and that is a fair division of labour rather than a deficiency in either.

FAQ

Is Elicit free? There is a free Basic tier. It carries limited usage for the Research Agent and Research Reports, with unlimited search across more than 138 million papers, unlimited summaries, unlimited chat with papers where full text is available, viewable sources and Zotero import. Paid tiers start at $11 per user/month billed as $132 annually.

Can Elicit run Python or R on my dataset? The public pages we examined describe search, screening, extraction, reports and figures pulled from papers. They do not describe a user-runnable Python or R code sandbox. If the task is a regression on your own file with the code retained, that is a different product category.

Does Elicit train on my data? The pricing page states "No training on your data by default" at the Enterprise tier, alongside SSO & SAML and enterprise controls. The pages we examined do not make an equivalent statement for the self-serve tiers, so we do not attribute one to them.

Does HumanPen Start Project do literature search as well? Partly. The page shows a PubMed search with every DOI checked, and lists literature review and evidence building as a use case. It does not publish a screening capacity figure, so if you need to screen 5,000 abstracts against criteria, that is Elicit's territory rather than a claim we can make here.
