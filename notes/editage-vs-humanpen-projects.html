Editage vs HumanPen Start Project: language polish versus a defensible result

Short answer

Editage sells human expertise at scale. Its page lists more than 20 expert services and AI tools — English editing, translation, publication support, graphical abstracts, an AI writing assistant, a literature review tool and an illustration tool — with two subject matter experts assigned to each project and unlimited re-editing inside defined windows.

HumanPen Start Project does not polish prose. It takes your data and documents, proposes a plan, and returns an analysis: a .docx report, 300 dpi figures, three-line tables, formatted references, editable .pptx with speaker notes, and the code.

The decision is usually about sequence, not preference. Editing improves a manuscript that already exists. If the tables are wrong, no amount of English editing makes them right.

The sequence problem nobody prices

Most rejected manuscripts are not rejected for grammar. They are rejected because the primary endpoint is unclear, the table does not add up, or the claim exceeds the design. Editing services cannot fix any of those, because fixing them means going back to the data.

This is why the two categories get confused in procurement. A department buys editing, the manuscript comes back beautifully written, and the reviewer still asks why the confidence intervals are missing. The money was spent at the wrong stage.

The reverse failure is just as real. A correct, well-documented analysis written in flat, repetitive prose reads as weaker than it is — and in a competitive field, that costs citations.

What each side actually controls

Editage's page is explicit that its value is human judgement layered on tooling: subject-matter experts, publication support, artwork including graphical abstracts, and re-editing windows that let you iterate after journal feedback. For authors whose English is a second or third language, that is not a luxury. It is the difference between a fair review and a hostile one.

HumanPen's page is explicit that its value is a chain: attach files, describe the need, approve a plan, then receive reports, charts and code after a second-AI check. Its own example is a re-analysis with a six-month death-or-dependency endpoint at 62.2% versus 63.5%, a difference that did not reach significance at P = 0.072, a sensitivity analysis for missing outcomes, and a first-round review that passed with all 11 references found one by one.

Read the two lists side by side and the boundary is obvious: one service edits the sentence, the other builds the sentence's factual basis.

Where they legitimately overlap

Both touch language, from opposite ends.

HumanPen's document tools rewrite passages while keeping numbers, units, formulas, citations, terminology and claim strength exactly as they were. That is useful when a paragraph is syntactically fine but reads like a machine wrote it, and it is deliberately conservative — the product page states that it does not introduce typos or grammar errors to defeat detectors.

Editage's editing improves readability, flow and technical accuracy through a human editor who knows the field. That is a different intervention, and for a Methods section that a reviewer will parse line by line, a domain expert's eye is hard to replace.

Cost structure, described rather than quoted

Both are usage-based, and neither maps cleanly onto "per paper". Editage prices by service and length, with tiers that bundle re-editing windows and support. HumanPen sells credits that do not expire and work in both the web app and the API; analyses are billed per job, and a project's plan is shown before work starts.

The honest comparison is not per-word versus per-credit. It is "how many rounds will this take". A manuscript that needs three substantive revisions costs more than one that needs a proofread, whichever model you buy.

Constraints worth knowing before you commit

  • HumanPen intake accepts Excel, CSV, Word, PDF and zip up to 5 GB total; Wind and CNKI data must be exported first; GPU training is not supported.
  • Projects are retained six months after the last activity with a reminder three days before expiry; each runs in an isolated sandbox, is never shared across users, and is not used for training.
  • HumanPen is not a submission-handling service: it does not manage journal portals, cover letters or reviewer correspondence.

When to choose Editage

  • The science is settled and the English is the risk.
  • You need artwork, a graphical abstract, or publication-support logistics.
  • Your institution requires a named human editor or a service invoice.

When to choose HumanPen Start Project

  • The data has not been analysed yet.
  • Tables and figures must be produced to a rendering standard, not just described.
  • You want the code delivered with the numbers so the analysis can be defended and re-run.

FAQ

Can I do both? Yes, and in this order: produce the analysis and the tables first, then edit the prose. Editing before the numbers are final wastes a revision cycle.

Does HumanPen improve academic English? It rewrites phrasing while holding numbers, citations and claim strength fixed. It is not a substitute for a subject-matter editor on a Methods section.

Does Editage run statistics? No. Its services are language, translation, artwork and publication support.

Which is right for a thesis deadline in two weeks? If the analysis exists, editing. If it does not, no editing service can compress the work that has not happened.
