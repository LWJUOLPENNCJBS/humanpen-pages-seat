Humata vs HumanPen Start Project: A Page Meter vs a Project That Ends in a Report

Humata is the better choice when the work is reading. You have PDFs — papers, reports, contracts, scanned appendices — and you want to ask questions across them and pull answers out without turning every page yourself. Its plans are metered in pages: the free tier allows up to 60 pages a month, Expert is $9.99/month for 500 pages a month with 3 users and $0.02 per additional page, and Team is $49 per user per month for 5,000 pages a month, 10 users and $0.01 per additional page.

HumanPen Start Project is the better choice when the work is computing. The PDFs are inputs, not the subject: you need the numbers inside the data behind them re-derived, checked and written up, and the deliverable is a document with tables, figures and code. It plans first, starts when you confirm, runs Python or R in an isolated sandbox, and delivers a .docx report, 300 dpi figures, three-line tables, slides with speaker notes and the code.

One product answers questions about a document. The other produces the document that answers the question.

How the workflows differ

1. Reading a corpus vs analysing a dataset

Humata's unit is the page. You upload documents, you ask, and the product works within what those pages contain. That is the right shape for a task whose difficulty is volume: a stack of papers you have to triage before a meeting, a technical annex nobody has finished, a folder of reports where the answer exists but nobody knows which file holds it. The output is an answer you can act on immediately.

HumanPen Start Project's unit is a project with a stated question. Attach a survey export, a codebook and the journal's table template, and the work is not "find the sentence" but "estimate the model, check it, and write it up". The page's own example makes the difference concrete: a secondary analysis of the International Stroke Trial with 19,435 patients in the full analysis set, 62.2% of the aspirin group and 63.5% of the control group dead or dependent at 6 months, OR 0.95 (0.89–1.00), P = 0.072. No amount of question-answering over the PDF of that trial produces those numbers; they have to be computed.

2. A conversation vs five stages

Humata's loop is a conversation. Ask, read the answer, ask again with a narrower question. It is cheap to iterate and it rewards a user who already knows what they are looking for.

HumanPen's loop is a sequence: Describe, Plan, In progress, In review, Delivered. The design decision is that plan comes before work and work starts when you confirm. If your request leaves something open, the project asks rather than guessing — the public example shows the system restating the brief (analysis set, endpoint, sensitivity analysis for about 150 missing outcomes) before it runs anything. Then a second AI reviews the output before delivery; the page shows the result as "Review passed (round 1)", with the endpoint matching, the populations adding up, a clean rerun reproducing the numbers and all 11 references found one by one.

For a question, a conversation is the right instrument. For a result that other people will rely on, a checkpoint is. The two loops are not competing versions of the same thing; they answer different risks.

3. Pages in vs pages out

The page meter has one behavioural effect worth naming, because it is neither a flaw nor a virtue. It makes consumption visible. On Expert, a bundle of documents that carries you past 500 pages in a month starts costing $0.02 a page, and you will notice. That is useful discipline for a reading workflow whose cost really does scale with pages.

HumanPen's constraint is capacity, not consumption: 5 GB total per project, whatever mix of spreadsheets, documents and archives you attach. It does not get more expensive because the appendix is long. The trade is that the constraint you manage is scoping — what is in the project, and what the plan says will be produced — rather than a monthly counter.

Uploading files and what comes back

Humata takes PDFs and returns answers and extracted material. Its pricing is expressed entirely in pages per month, which tells you what the product considers scarce. If your input is a single journal article, the free allowance covers it. If your input is a scanned archive that needs OCR, that control is listed from the Team plan up, so check the plan table before assuming the free tier will handle it.

HumanPen Start Project takes Excel, CSV, Word, PDF and zip up to 5 GB total and returns a specified package: a .docx report, charts at 300 dpi to journal standards, three-line tables and captions, a .pptx with speaker notes, and the code that produced the numbers. That last item is the one researchers underrate. A report tells you what the answer is; the code tells you how it was reached, which is the difference between a draft you can defend in review and a draft you have to re-derive before you can defend it.

The two also differ in what "done" means for mixed inputs. A folder containing twelve PDFs, three spreadsheets and a Word template is a natural unit for HumanPen — one project, one plan, one delivery. For Humata the same folder is a set of documents to question; the answers are useful, but they are inputs to your own writing, not the writing.

Price shape and governance

Humata's shape is a subscription with a page allowance and a per-page rate beyond it: 60 pages a month on Free, 500 on Expert at $9.99/month with 3 users, 5,000 on Team at $49 per user per month with 10 users, and $0.02 or $0.01 per additional page respectively. That is a good fit for reading work, where consumption tracks the number of pages you actually need to get through, and a slightly awkward fit for a project whose page count is unknown at the start.

HumanPen Start Project has no per-page meter. The published constraint is capacity, 5 GB total per project, plus the scope in the plan you confirm. We are not quoting a price for it here because the pages we read do not publish a price list for this work, and a number invented for a comparison post is worse than no number.

On controls, the two answer different questions. Humata's plan table lists OCR and department- and folder-level permissions from the Team plan up, with a SOC-2 certificate and an uptime SLA shown on Enterprise — the vocabulary of a team buying a document system. HumanPen's page answers the ethics-committee version: transport encryption, each project in its own isolated sandbox, no sharing across users, no use for training, deletion at any time, retention for 6 months after the last activity with a reminder 3 days before expiry.

Neither list substitutes for the other, and neither is a claim about the other's security posture. We did not audit either vendor's data-processing agreement for this piece.

When to choose which

Choose Humata if the bottleneck is reading: you have documents and a deadline, the answer already exists somewhere in them, and you need it extracted rather than computed. The free tier's 60 pages a month is enough to test that on a real paper, and the page meter stays predictable for a reading workload.

Choose HumanPen Start Project if the bottleneck is producing: the data needs a model run on it, the tables need to be built to a journal's style, and someone will ask how a figure was generated. The plan-first sequence, the second-AI review and the delivered code are for that situation.

Use both if your project has both bottlenecks, which most empirical papers do. Read and triage the literature in Humata to decide what to cite and what to discard; then hand the dataset and the method to HumanPen Start Project, confirm the plan, and take delivery of the report, figures and code. The reading tool decides what the paper says about the field; the project tool decides what the paper says about the data.

The mismatch to avoid is expecting either to do the other's job — asking a document-Q&A tool to recompute a hazard ratio, or asking a project tool to summarise a folder of PDFs you have not read.

FAQ

Is Humata free? Yes, up to 60 pages a month. Expert at $9.99/month raises that to 500 pages a month with 3 users and charges $0.02 per additional page; Team at $49 per user per month covers 5,000 pages a month, 10 users and $0.01 per additional page.

What happens if I go over my Humata page allowance? Extra pages are billed per page: $0.02 on Expert and $0.01 on Team, per the pricing page. There is no equivalent meter on HumanPen Start Project, which is scoped by project and by a 5 GB total intake.

Can Humata run a regression on the data behind my PDFs? The public pages we examined describe asking questions, summarising and extracting across uploaded PDFs. They do not describe a statistical execution environment or a code deliverable. If the table inside the PDF has to be re-estimated from the underlying data, that is a different job.

How large a project can I attach to HumanPen Start Project? Excel, CSV, Word, PDF and zip up to 5 GB total. Analysis runs on Python or R; analyses originally done in SPSS or Stata are redone in Python or R; Wind and CNKI data must be exported first; GPU training is not supported.
