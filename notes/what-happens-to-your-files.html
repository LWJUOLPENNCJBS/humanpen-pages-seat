What happens to your files after delivery? Retention, isolation and training at Julius, Posit, Humata, Zotero and HumanPen

Three questions get merged into one whenever a vendor says "private", and they should not be. How long does the material stay? Who else can reach it while it is there? Is it used to train anything? A tool can answer well on one and say nothing about the other two, and a single "privacy" verdict hides exactly the part a supervisor or an ethics committee will ask about.

The differences are visible once you separate them. Julius lists 7-day file storage across Free, Plus, Pro and Business, with permanent file storage on Enterprise. Posit's page for Posit Assistant states zero data retention — code and data are never stored or used for training — and that statement is about that product. Humata lists a SOC-2 certificate from the Team plan upward but, on the pricing page examined, no retention period. Zotero's storage is a pool you keep rather than a processing window. HumanPen Start Project states all three: encryption in transit, an isolated sandbox per project, no cross-user sharing, no use for training, deletion at any time, and retention of six months after the last activity with a reminder three days before expiry.

So the useful comparison is a three-column one. Read each row separately, and the tool that looks strongest on retention may be silent on training, or vice versa.

Julius: a seven-day window, and a permanent tier

Julius is the clearest example of retention as a product feature, because the length of the window is printed in the feature table. File access is listed as 7-day file storage on Free, Plus, Pro and Business, and permanent file storage on Enterprise. The same table places SOC 2 Type II compliance, two-factor authentication, user roles and permissions, and single sign-on at Business and Enterprise.

The plan prices are $20 per month for Plus, $16 per month on annual billing, $45 per month for Pro, $37 on annual, and $450 per month for Business, $375 on annual. Business also carries 60,000 credits a month and room for up to 50 team members, which is the tier where the governance features appear.

What the page does not spell out is what the seventh day means in practice — whether the files are deleted, or merely no longer accessible in the workspace. That distinction matters for a records policy, and it is worth asking directly rather than inferring from the label. What is clear is the shape of the offer: a short working window for individuals, and a permanent one for enterprises that need it.

Posit: zero retention, stated narrowly and precisely

The Posit page makes the strongest single statement of the group, and it is worth quoting rather than paraphrasing: "Zero data retention: your code and data are never stored or used for training." The same page describes Posit Assistant as a conversational AI agent embedded in RStudio that reads your live session context and answers from your actual data.

The scope is the point. That sentence appears on the Posit Assistant page, describing Posit Assistant. Posit AI is available for $20 per month with a free trial, addable to any edition, and RStudio Desktop Pro is $1204.00 per year for a commercial licence with priority support. The open-source IDE is free under AGPL v3.

For a researcher whose concern is "does my loaded data frame leave my machine and end up in a training set", this is the most directly relevant sentence in the comparison. For a researcher whose concern is "how long does a vendor keep an uploaded archive", Posit is not an upload tool in that sense — the IDE runs where you run it, and the AI layer's promise is about not storing the session.

Humata: controls on the plan, silence on the clock

Humata's pricing page is organised around capacity and controls. The Team plan, at $49 per user per month, lists department-level and folder-level permissions, OCR and response personalisation; a SOC-2 certificate, an uptime SLA and custom page allowances appear on the higher tiers. Free covers 60 pages a month, Expert $9.99 per month covers 500 pages with $0.02 per additional page, and Team covers 5,000 pages with $0.01 per additional page.

Those are real governance signals — a SOC-2 certificate is an audited control set, and folder-level permissions determine who inside an organisation can reach a document. What the page we examined does not describe is a retention period: there is no statement of how long an uploaded PDF remains available, nor of a deletion schedule. Nor does it describe a training policy for uploaded content.

That is not a criticism so much as a gap to close with a question. If your institution's data-management plan requires a stated retention window, the pricing page will not supply it.

Zotero: retention by subscription

Zotero's model is different in kind. Storage holds your PDFs, images, web snapshots and other files, synchronises them across devices, shares attachments in group libraries and makes them available through the online library. The tiers are 300 MB free, a paid tier at $20 per year, 6 GB at $60 per year and unlimited at $120 per year.

That is retention as a subscription rather than a policy window: files stay as long as the storage holds them and you keep them, and group libraries draw on the owner's account. The storage page describes capacity, synchronisation and sharing; it does not describe a deletion schedule, nor a training policy for library content.

For the custody question, Zotero is strong where a library is the asset: the material is yours, it persists, and it moves with you. For the "how long does a vendor keep a working file" question, there is no clock to read, because the library is not a processing window — it is the place the files live.

HumanPen: all three answers on one page

HumanPen's project page states the three axes in a single paragraph, which is unusual and makes it easy to audit. Encryption in transit. Every project runs in its own isolated sandbox. Never shared across users. Never used for training. Can be deleted completely at any time. Retention is six months after the last activity, with a reminder three days before expiry, and deletion available whenever you want it.

The isolation claim is the one that differs most from a workspace account. A sandbox per project means the unit of containment is the commission, not the user: two projects by the same person do not share a working environment, and nothing is pooled across customers. For a review built on an unpublished manuscript plus a dataset, that is the sentence a data-management plan wants, because it names the boundary rather than the intention.

The retention claim is also testable. Six months is a stated number, the reminder is a stated behaviour, and deletion at any time is a stated right, so a lab can write "retained six months after last activity, deletable on request" into a protocol and check it later. The page's billing model reinforces the same logic: work is metered by actual usage, failed tasks are not charged, and a plan carries no price up front, so nothing about the retention window depends on a subscription tier.

Three decisions

If your files must not outlive the job, start with the shortest window. Julius states 7-day file storage on Free, Plus, Pro and Business, the shortest stated window in this group; Enterprise is the exception, with permanent file storage. HumanPen offers a different control: delete at any time, with a six-month default and a reminder before expiry. The first suits "clear the workspace by default"; the second suits "keep it while the project is live, then decide".

If the concern is training, read the sentence and its scope. Posit's statement — code and data are never stored or used for training — is explicit, and it sits on the Posit Assistant page, so it covers that assistant. HumanPen states never used for training as a property of the project sandbox. For Humata and Zotero, the pages examined do not describe a training policy, which is a question to put in writing rather than an assumption either way.

If the files are a library rather than a job, retention is the point. Zotero's 300 MB free and paid tiers from $20 per year are built to hold material across years, and the value comes from persistence. Applying a processing-window policy to a library would be a category error; applying a library model to a commissioned analysis would leave client data sitting in a workspace long after the deliverable shipped.

The mistake is to collapse the three columns into a feeling. A tool with a SOC-2 certificate can still be silent on training; a tool with zero retention can be narrow in scope; a tool with a six-month window can offer deletion on demand. Score each column separately, and ask for the missing cell in writing.

FAQ

Does Julius keep files longer than seven days? The pricing page lists 7-day file storage on Free, Plus, Pro and Business, and permanent file storage on Enterprise. What happens at the end of the seven days — deletion or loss of access — is not spelled out on the page we examined.

Does Posit's zero-retention promise cover every Posit product? The sentence "Zero data retention: your code and data are never stored or used for training" appears on the Posit Assistant page and describes Posit Assistant. Posit AI is $20 per month and can be added to any edition; RStudio Desktop Pro is $1204.00 per year with a commercial licence.

How long does HumanPen keep a project? Six months after the last activity. You are reminded three days before expiry, and you can delete the project at any time, which ends the retention period early.

Can I delete a HumanPen project before the six months are up? Yes. The project page states that a project can be deleted completely at any time; the six-month window is the default, not a minimum commitment.
