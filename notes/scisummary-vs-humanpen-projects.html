SciSummary vs HumanPen Start Project: reading papers faster versus producing a result

Short answer

SciSummary compresses papers so you can read more of them. Its page describes breaking a paper into clearly labelled sections — abstract, methods, results, conclusion — supporting bulk summarization that synthesizes several papers into one summary, and interpreting p-values, confidence intervals and effect sizes. It also states it is the official AI provider for the ACM Digital Library and was founded in 2023.

HumanPen Start Project does not summarize the literature for you. It takes your data and documents, proposes a plan, and returns an analysis with a report, figures, tables, formatted references and editable slides.

The distinction that decides the purchase: a summary tells you what a paper says; a delivery tells you what your data says. Those are different bottlenecks, and most researchers have both at different times.

Summaries and computations are different verbs

SciSummary's most useful claim is not speed, it is structure. The page argues that generic assistants produce a blur, while its output separates methods from results so you know which paragraph a number came from. It also says it interprets reported statistics — p-values, confidence intervals, effect sizes — in language you can use.

That is interpretation of what the authors computed. It is not computation. If a paper reports an odds ratio without the confidence interval you need, or if your own dataset needs the same model fitted, a summarizer cannot supply it.

HumanPen's side of that line is explicit on the project page: analysis runs on Python or R, and analyses originally done in SPSS or Stata are performed in Python or R. The page's worked example goes further and shows what "delivered" means — a six-month death-or-dependency endpoint at 62.2% versus 63.5%, a difference that did not reach significance at P = 0.072, a sensitivity analysis for missing outcomes, and a first-round review that passed with all 11 references found one by one.

Notice that last clause. A summarizer will happily produce a paragraph citing a paper; the delivery pipeline treats citation integrity as a check that can fail.

Where the two overlap, and why it matters less than it looks

Both touch "the literature". SciSummary reads it for you; HumanPen reads what you attach, including PDFs, and produces references in the report.

The overlap is real but shallow. If your literature review is the deliverable — a scoping report, a reading-based seminar paper — SciSummary plus your own writing is a reasonable stack. If the deliverable must contain a table that a reviewer will recompute, summarizing is not the step that gets you there.

Throughput versus traceability

Bulk summarization raises throughput: more papers screened per hour, fewer abandoned reading lists. That is a genuine gain at the screening stage of a review.

The cost is traceability, and it appears later. When a reviewer asks "on what basis did you choose this model specification", a summary of someone else's paper is not an answer. What answers it is code, a stated model, and a table of coefficients — which is what HumanPen's delivery includes, along with editable slides and notes for the defence.

Honest limits on the HumanPen side

  • GPU training is not supported, so model training is out of scope.
  • Wind and CNKI data must be exported before upload.
  • Projects are kept for six months after the last activity with a reminder three days before expiry; you can delete them at any time, and each project runs in an isolated sandbox that is never shared across users or used for training.
  • Intake is capped at 5 GB total per project, which is generous for text and tabular work but not for raw imaging pipelines.

When to choose SciSummary

  • You are screening a large pile of papers and need to triage fast.
  • You want a structured explanation of a methods section you do not follow.
  • Your output is a literature-based document, not a data result.

When to choose HumanPen Start Project

  • You have data and no analysis.
  • You need figures at a rendering standard a journal will accept.
  • You are defending a re-analysis and want the code alongside it.

FAQ

Can SciSummary run my regression? No. It interprets statistics that are already reported in a paper.

Can HumanPen summarize a hundred papers into a literature review? It processes what you attach and delivers a report; it is not a bulk summarizer for screening.

Do the two compete? Only at the intake boundary. One reads other people's results, the other computes yours.
