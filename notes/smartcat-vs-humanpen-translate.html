Smartcat vs HumanPen Translate: A Localization Program vs a Single Document

Short answer

Choose Smartcat when the unit of work is a program rather than a file: a steady stream of content — website, video, audio, image, subtitle, Excel, InDesign, XLIFF, PDF, document — moving into several markets, produced by people plus AI coworkers inside a translation management system with a CAT tool. Its page describes "The AI platform for global content | All-in-one solution", is enterprise-oriented, and offers "Start free trial".

Choose HumanPen Translate when the unit of work is one document and the person doing it is you. It accepts PDF, Word (docx), PowerPoint (pptx), Excel (xlsx), EPUB, HTML and plain text and returns the same format you sent, with charts, formulas, hyperlinks and page structure parsed and rich-text styling preserved. It covers 12 languages at 10 credits per 1,000 words, from the same balance as the humanize, condense and citation tools.

Different jobs under adjacent labels. Smartcat's page positions it as an enterprise localisation platform and TMS; it does not describe a per-document, pay-as-you-go translator for academic or individual documents. A program and a file are not rival answers to one question; the choice is mostly whether you are running an operation or finishing a paper.

Where the two tools actually diverge

1. A programme and a document are different objects

A translation management system exists because content arrives continuously and in many shapes. Smartcat's page reads as an operating environment: content types that map to specialist workflows, AI coworkers, a CAT tool, a trial for organisations. Nothing in that description is about one file — it is about the throughput of many files through a process with roles.

HumanPen Translate is the opposite shape. One upload, one returned file, one price computed on word count at 10 credits per 1,000 words. There is no memory to build, no vendor to assign, no stage to approve. That narrowness is the design, not a missing feature list.

The practical consequence: if a solo author were given a TMS, most of it would sit unused; if a localisation team ran on per-file uploads, the coordination that makes their work consistent would evaporate.

2. Who is in the loop while the work happens

Smartcat's page names AI coworkers and a CAT tool. Both presuppose a division of labour — a system that drafts or suggests, a person who reviews, tooling that keeps terminology steady across a programme.

HumanPen Translate's page describes no such loop. It states an input, an output, a price and a retention window: job files kept 7 days then removed, job record stays. No review step, no memory, no terminology base. For a document you alone are responsible for, that absence is the feature; for a programme that must not drift between releases, it is the machinery you cannot do without.

3. Two lists of "types" that are not the same kind of list

Both pages contain a list that includes Excel, which makes them look closer than they are. The lists answer different questions.

Smartcat's list — PDF, document, website, video, audio, image, subtitle, Excel, InDesign, XLIFF — is a routing taxonomy. It tells you which specialist translator a piece of content is handed to inside a programme. "Excel translator" there means a workflow exists for spreadsheet-shaped content.

HumanPen Translate's list — PDF, DOCX, PPTX, XLSX, EPUB, HTML, plain text — is a promise about the return leg. The workbook comes back as a workbook, with formulas, charts and hyperlinks parsed and rich-text styling preserved, in the format you sent. The unit is fidelity inside the file, not routing between files.

That is why the two products can both say "Excel" and mean different things. If you need a spreadsheet translated inside a multi-market release schedule, routing is the question. If you need the workbook back with its formulas intact so you can keep working in it tonight, fidelity is.

When to choose which (or use both)

Choose Smartcat if you are running localisation rather than performing a translation: multiple content types, multiple markets, more than one person touching the work, a need for CAT tooling and a managed process, and an organisation that can adopt a platform. "Start free trial" is the sane way to test whether that fit is real.

Choose HumanPen Translate if the work is a document you own: a paper going to a journal in another language, a report for a partner, a deck for a conference, a workbook for a colleague abroad. The triggers are the objects inside the file — formulas, charts, hyperlinks, heading structure, rich-text styling — plus a cost you can calculate per document and a balance that does not expire.

Use both, at different altitudes. A research group with a publishing programme might run institutional content through a TMS and still send individual manuscripts to HumanPen Translate, because a single paper does not need a workflow, it needs a file back. One is the factory, the other the desk.

FAQ

Q. Is Smartcat a per-document translator? A. Its page positions Smartcat as "The AI platform for global content", a translation management system with AI coworkers and CAT tooling, oriented to enterprises. It does not describe a per-document, pay-as-you-go academic document translator.

Q. Does HumanPen Translate manage a translation team? A. No. Its page describes one upload and one returned file, with no vendor assignment, translation memory, CAT tool or review roles. Those belong to the TMS side of this comparison.

Q. Both mention Excel. Is that the same capability? A. No. Smartcat's list describes content types routed to specialist translators inside a programme; HumanPen Translate's describes formats returned in the same shape, with formulas, charts and hyperlinks preserved.

Q. What does HumanPen Translate cost? A. 1,000 words costs 10 credits. Credits never expire, work in the web app and the API, and share one balance with the humanize, condense and citation tools. New accounts get 100 free credits, and failed or cancelled document jobs are never charged.

Q. What happens to the files after the job? A. Job files are kept 7 days and then removed; the job record stays. Data is encrypted in transit, never used for training, and deletable.
