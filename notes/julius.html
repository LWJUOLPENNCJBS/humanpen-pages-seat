Julius AI vs HumanPen Start Project: A Seven-Day Workspace vs a Six-Month Project

Julius is the stronger product when the work is broad and fast. It presents itself as one AI workspace — analyse data and research, create presentations and reports, build websites, and generate images and video — and it sells that breadth through credits: Plus at $20/month ($16 annual) with 2,000 credits monthly and 16 GB of RAM, Pro at $45/month ($37 annual) with 5,000 credits monthly and 32 GB of RAM, Business at $450/month ($375 annual) with 60,000 credits monthly and up to 50 team members.

HumanPen Start Project is the stronger product when the work has to end in a defensible document. It takes a described task and attached data, returns a plan, waits for your confirmation, runs the analysis in Python or R inside an isolated sandbox, has a second AI review the result, and delivers a .docx report, 300 dpi figures, three-line tables, a .pptx with speaker notes and the code that produced the numbers.

The deciding difference for most academic and clinical work is not the feature list. It is one line in Julius's plan table: file access is 7-day file storage on Free, Plus, Pro and Business, and permanent file storage only on Enterprise. HumanPen keeps a project for 6 months after the last activity. One is a workspace you work in this week; the other is a record you can still open when a co-author asks in the spring.

How the workflows differ

1. Breadth of output vs depth of one output

Julius's page is organised around range. The same subscription covers a chart, a deck, a dashboard, a website and a video, and the framing is explicit: "One AI workspace for analysis and creation". That is a real advantage if your week contains six kinds of artefact and you would rather not pay for six tools; the marginal cost of the next format is close to zero.

HumanPen Start Project is organised around one chain of custody. A single project carries the brief, the files, the plan, the analysis, the review and the delivery. The worked example on the page is a secondary analysis of the International Stroke Trial: 19,435 patients in the full analysis set, a primary endpoint of death or dependency at 6 months, about 150 missing outcomes handled with a sensitivity analysis, 62.2% in the aspirin group against 63.5% in the control group, OR 0.95 (0.89–1.00), P = 0.072, and references traced through PubMed with every DOI checked.

"I need a deck by Thursday" and "I need a number I can defend in review" are not the same request, and a product tuned for the first does not automatically satisfy the second.

2. A credit balance vs a confirmed plan

Julius meters work in credits: 2,000 a month on Plus, 5,000 on Pro, 60,000 on Business, plus 100 daily refresh credits on Plus and free daily credits on Free. A credit balance is a good instrument for exploration — spend a little, look at the result, spend more — and it makes cost legible before a long session.

HumanPen's control is a checkpoint rather than a balance. The stages are Describe, Plan, In progress, In review, Delivered, and the rule stated on the page is "Plan first, start when you confirm". Before anything runs, the project restates the brief — in the public example, the analysis set, the endpoint and the sensitivity analysis — and waits. If your request leaves a decision open, it asks instead of choosing for you.

A credit meter rewards you for knowing when to stop spending. A plan checkpoint forces you to know what you are asking for before the first number is computed. Researchers who already know their specification will find the checkpoint mildly redundant; researchers who have rerun an analysis because the population definition was wrong will not.

3. Seven days vs six months

This is the line to read twice.

Julius's plan table states the storage window plainly: Free, Plus, Pro and Business all carry 7-day file storage, and permanent file storage appears only on Enterprise. That is coherent for a workspace — the platform is where work happens, the output leaves, storage is not the product. If you export on Friday, seven days is invisible. If you come back after a holiday, a review round or a co-author's sabbatical, it is not.

HumanPen's stated retention is 6 months after the last activity, with a reminder 3 days before expiry. The two products answer different questions. Julius answers "how much workspace do we hold for an active account". HumanPen answers "how long does a finished project stay retrievable".

For a thesis chapter, a grant resubmission or a clinical report queried months later, the second question is the one that bites. It is also the cheapest thing to check before you commit: read the storage line, not the credits line.

Uploading files and what comes back

Julius's page describes analysing files and creating charts and presentations on Plus, and analysing spreadsheets, documents and data files on Pro, with 16 GB of RAM on Plus and 32 GB of RAM on Pro. Business adds data connectors — Postgres, BigQuery, Snowflake and more — with no table limit in data connections. What the page we examined does not describe is a total intake ceiling per project, or a code artefact handed back with the analysis.

HumanPen Start Project publishes its intake in one line: Excel, CSV, Word, PDF and zip, up to 5 GB total. The output is as concrete — a .docx report, charts at 300 dpi to journal standards, three-line tables and captions, a .pptx with speaker notes, and the code. The page is equally explicit about boundaries: analysis runs on Python or R, SPSS and Stata work is redone in Python or R, Wind and CNKI data must be exported first, GPU training is not yet supported. A tool that states what it will not do is easier to plan around.

Price shape and governance

Julius prices in tiers with a credit allowance attached to each: $0 on Free with daily credits; $20/month or $16/month billed annually on Plus with 2,000 credits monthly and 16 GB of RAM; $45/month or $37/month billed annually on Pro with 5,000 credits monthly and 32 GB of RAM; $450/month or $375/month billed annually on Business with 60,000 credits monthly and up to 50 team members. Enterprise is priced on contact and is the only tier where file storage is permanent.

Governance follows the same ladder: SOC 2 Type II, two-factor authentication, user roles and permissions and single sign-on on Business and Enterprise; connectors starting with Google Drive, OneDrive and SharePoint on Free and Plus, and adding Snowflake, BigQuery and Postgres on Pro and Business. For a department buying fifty seats, that ladder is the part of the page that matters.

HumanPen's published controls are written for a different reader — the ethics committee rather than the procurement office: transport encryption, an isolated sandbox per project, no sharing across users, no use for training, deletion at any time, 6-month retention with a 3-day reminder. The developer page adds the machine-readable version: an API at https://api.humanpen.net/v1, a plan confirmation carrying a spec_version, short-lived signed URLs for the delivery zip, and webhooks that hold only the project and its state.

We are not quoting a HumanPen price here: it sells credits rather than subscription seats, and a number invented for a comparison post is worse than no number.

When to choose which

Choose Julius if the deliverable is varied and the deadline is close: a dashboard for a lab meeting, a deck from a results table, a short report, a project site. The breadth is genuine, the credit tiers are easy to reason about, and 16 GB or 32 GB of RAM is stated plainly enough to plan against. If outputs leave the platform within the week, the 7-day window costs you nothing.

Choose HumanPen Start Project if the deliverable is a document someone will audit: a paper's methods and results, a clinical report, a grant's preliminary analysis. The plan checkpoint, the second-AI review, the 6-month retention and the delivered code are aimed at the moment six weeks later when a reviewer asks how a figure was produced. The 5 GB total intake also suits a project that is one dataset plus its documentation, rather than a stream of small files.

Use both if your work has a wide front and a narrow back. Explore and present in Julius — try three cuts of the data, build the deck, publish the project page — then move the dataset and the final specification into HumanPen Start Project, confirm the plan, and take delivery of the report, figures and code that go into the submission. The exploratory tool decides what is interesting; the project tool decides what is defensible.

The mismatch to avoid is treating either as a substitute for the other's strongest property. A workspace built for many output types is not also an archive; a project tool built for one audited deliverable is not the fastest route to a website.

FAQ

Is Julius AI free? Yes. Free is $0 with free daily credits. Plus is $20/month, or $16/month billed annually, with 2,000 credits monthly and 16 GB of RAM; Pro is $45/month, or $37/month billed annually, with 5,000 credits monthly and 32 GB of RAM.

Does Julius keep my files? Its plan table states 7-day file storage on Free, Plus, Pro and Business, and permanent file storage on Enterprise. If a file needs to be there next month on a self-serve plan, that is the line to check before you upload.

Can Julius run Python on my dataset? The page describes analysing files and creating charts and presentations, and on Pro analysing spreadsheets, documents and data files, with 16 GB of RAM on Plus and 32 GB of RAM on Pro. It does not describe submitting your own Python or R script and getting the code back as a delivered artefact, so we claim neither way.

How large a project can I attach to HumanPen Start Project? Excel, CSV, Word, PDF and zip up to 5 GB total. Analysis runs on Python or R; SPSS or Stata work is redone in Python or R; Wind and CNKI data must be exported first; GPU training is not supported.
