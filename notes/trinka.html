Trinka vs HumanPen Start Project: The Sentence Layer vs the Analysis Layer

Trinka is the better choice when the manuscript already exists and the problem is how it reads. It is a writing assistant with an unusually wide set of checks attached: grammar and language, a paraphraser, a consistency check, translation, citation quality check and formatter, journal finder, AI detector and plagiarism. The entry plan is free — Basic is listed at 0 per month — and the privacy-first tier, Confidential Data, is $300 annually, or $41.67 per month.

HumanPen Start Project is the better choice when the manuscript does not exist yet because the numbers behind it have not been produced. You describe the task and attach the data, the project returns a plan, work starts only when you confirm it, the analysis runs in Python or R, a second AI reviews the result, and what comes back is a .docx report, journal-grade figures, three-line tables and the code.

They sit at different points on the same pipeline, which is why the useful question is not "which is better" but "which end of the work is stuck". If your results section is written and clumsy, Trinka is aimed at exactly that. If your results section is empty because the dataset has not been analysed, no amount of line editing will fill it.

How the workflows differ

1. Trinka starts after the draft; HumanPen starts before it

The shape of Trinka's product is a document that already says something. Its tools are all refinements of expression: a grammar checker, a paraphraser, a consistency check for terminology and formatting, translation, a citation quality check and formatter, a journal finder, an AI detector and a plagiarism check. Each one assumes prose on the page and asks whether that prose is correct, consistent, well-cited, well-placed or original.

That is a lot of work compressed into one subscription, and for a non-native English speaker it is the work that delays submission. The tool list reads like a pre-submission checklist rather than an analysis environment.

HumanPen Start Project begins one step earlier, where there is nothing to proofread. A project opens with a description of the task and the files, and the first artefact produced is not text but a plan: what will be analysed, which population, which endpoint, which sensitivity analysis. The public example on the page is a secondary analysis of the International Stroke Trial, and the plan step restates it — a full analysis set of 19,435 patients, a primary endpoint of death or dependency at 6 months, and about 150 missing outcomes handled with a sensitivity analysis — before anything runs.

The distinction matters because the two products fail in different ways. A wrong population definition is not a language error, so a prose check will not surface it; a written plan can, before anything runs.

2. Suggestions vs a specification

Trinka's unit of interaction is the suggestion. You accept or reject a change, sentence by sentence, and the judgement stays with you throughout. Nothing happens to your document that you did not click on. For an author who wants final control over every comma — and most authors do — that is the right contract.

HumanPen's unit of interaction is the plan. The stages are Describe, Plan, In progress, In review and Delivered, and the rule on the page is "Plan first, start when you confirm". If the brief leaves a decision open, the project asks rather than choosing quietly. Before delivery, a second AI reviews the work; the page shows the review output as "Review passed (round 1)", with the checks named — primary endpoint matches, every analysis population adds up, a clean rerun reproduces the results exactly, all references found one by one.

The trade is legibility for leverage. A suggestion tool leaves you in full possession of the text and takes an hour a section. A plan-and-deliver tool takes the job off your desk and asks the harder thing first: specify it well enough that a stranger could execute it.

3. Two different definitions of "checking the references"

Both products touch citations, but not the same way.

Trinka lists a citation quality check and a citation formatter among its tools. The framing is formatting and quality: does the citation conform, is the style consistent, is the entry well-formed.

HumanPen's example handles references by retrieval: a PubMed search is run and every DOI is checked one by one, with the two cited trials — IST and CAST, both Lancet 1997 — named on the page. The review step then reports that all 11 references were found, one by one.

Formatting a reference and confirming that the reference exists are different jobs, and a paper needs both. A perfectly formatted citation to a paper that says something else is still a retraction waiting to happen.

Uploading files and what comes back

Trinka's intake is a document, and its output is that same document, corrected. Nothing else leaves the system, because nothing else was ever asked for.

HumanPen publishes its intake in a single line, which is unusually specific for this category: Excel, CSV, Word, PDF and zip up to 5 GB total.

The output is equally concrete — a .docx report, charts at 300 dpi to journal standards, three-line tables and captions to standard, a .pptx deck with speaker notes, and the code. The page is also explicit about writing: its de-AI-tone step keeps numbers and citations exactly as they are, and the before-and-after example shows the same two percentages and the same P value rephrased into a shorter sentence rather than rounded or restated.

If your problem is that the file you have is badly written, Trinka's output is the file you want. If your problem is that the file you have is a spreadsheet, HumanPen's output is the file you want.

Price shape and governance

Trinka publishes a plan table with four columns, and the numbers it renders are worth reading exactly as they appear. Basic is 0 per month. Confidential Data is $300 annually, which the table also expresses as $41.67 per month, and it is the column the page places under its most-secure heading, for sensitive and proprietary content.

Premium and Premium Plus sit between those two columns, and here we have to be precise: the plan table did not render prices to our capture. The columns and their feature lists were present; no figure appeared next to them. We would rather record that hole in our reading than fill it with a plausible-looking number.

The writing side is metered in words rather than currency: Writing Assistance is listed from 5,000 words upwards, with the paid columns showing "Full Access". The compliance labels — SOC2, HIPAA, FERPA, GDPR and ISO — sit on the institutional and enterprise side, which tells you who that story is written for.

HumanPen sells credits rather than subscription seats, so this comparison quotes no per-month figure for it. What it does publish is a set of controls written for the ethics committee rather than the procurement office: encryption in transit, an isolated sandbox per project, no sharing across users, no use for training, deletion at any time, retention of 6 months after the last activity with a reminder 3 days before expiry, and revisions scoped and confirmed before work starts.

When to choose which

Choose Trinka if the draft is done and the remaining work is language, consistency, citation form and submission fit. The free Basic tier makes it a low-risk default for a student, the tool list covers most of the pre-submission checklist in one place, and the Confidential Data plan at $300 annually answers the sensitive-text case.

Choose HumanPen Start Project if the deliverable is a document whose numbers do not exist yet: a paper's methods and results, a clinical report, a grant's preliminary analysis, a reproduction of a published table. The plan checkpoint, the second-AI review, the delivered code and the 6-month retention are all aimed at the reviewer who asks, weeks later, how a figure was produced. The 5 GB total intake suits a dataset plus its documentation rather than a single manuscript.

Use both if your project has both holes. Run the analysis in HumanPen Start Project, confirm the plan, take delivery of the report, figures, tables and code — then put the manuscript through Trinka for the language pass, the consistency check and the citation formatting. One product produces the evidence, the other polishes the prose that carries it, and the handoff is a .docx in both directions.

The mismatch to avoid is buying a checker to solve an analysis problem. A grammar checker applied to an empty results section produces a beautifully written empty results section.

FAQ

Is Trinka free? Yes, on the entry plan. Basic is listed at 0 per month. Confidential Data is $300 annually, or $41.67 per month. Premium and Premium Plus are listed as columns in the same table, but the table did not render prices to our capture, so we do not quote a figure for either.

Does Trinka check citations? It lists a citation quality check and a citation formatter among its tools. What a check verifies beyond form and consistency is not described in the pages we examined, so we make no claim either way.

Can Trinka analyse my dataset? The pages we examined describe writing assistance and academic-integrity tools applied to text. They do not describe running a statistical analysis on a dataset, and they do not describe returning analysis code as a deliverable. We therefore neither claim nor deny that capability — we only report what the pages say.

How large a project can I attach to HumanPen Start Project? Excel, CSV, Word, PDF and zip, up to 5 GB total. Analysis runs on Python or R; SPSS or Stata work is redone in Python or R; Wind and CNKI data must be exported first; GPU training is not supported.
