Deepnote vs HumanPen Start Project: The Notebook You Drive vs the Report You Approve

Deepnote is the better tool when the analysis is yours to write. It is a collaborative notebook: you open a project, write Python or SQL in cells, run them, and share the result with the people who need to see it. The Free plan includes unlimited Basic machines with 5 GB RAM, 2 vCPU; the Team plan is $39 per editor per month billed yearly, with the pricing page showing -20% against the monthly rate; and the project machines in the page's own examples run at 4 vCPU / 8 GB on Python 3.12.

HumanPen Start Project is the better tool when you would rather not drive the keyboard. You describe what you need, attach the files — Excel, CSV, Word, PDF or zip, up to 5 GB total — read a plan, and confirm it. Only then does the work start. It runs the analysis in Python or R, has the result reviewed by a second AI, and delivers a .docx report, journal-standard figures, three-line tables, a .pptx with speaker notes, and the code.

The difference is not speed or power but who does the work: in Deepnote a person writes the analysis and the notebook is the product; in HumanPen the person writes the brief and approves a plan, and the deliverable arrives already reviewed.

How the workflows differ

1. The blank cell versus the written plan

Deepnote hands you a machine and a notebook. That is not a criticism — it is the product. The environment is already there when you sit down: the kernel, the packages, the connection, the chart. You start typing, and the analysis takes the shape you give it.

HumanPen Start Project hands you a form and a conversation. You attach the data, say what you want, and the first thing you get back is not a chart but a restatement of the job. Before anything runs, the plan reads back that the full analysis set is 19,435 patients, that the primary endpoint is death or dependency at 6 months, and that about 150 missing outcomes will get a sensitivity analysis. You confirm, and only then does the run begin.

That first artefact — plan versus cell — determines everything downstream. With a notebook, you can write the right code for the wrong question and find out three days later. With a plan, a wrong question is catchable before a line is executed, but nothing starts until you have decided.

2. What "shared" attaches to

Deepnote's collaboration is attached to the notebook: several people can work in the same project, look at the same cells and argue about the same output. The pricing shows the model — the paid tier is charged per editor per month, billed yearly, at $39.

HumanPen Start Project has no shared canvas. The collaboration is a contract: a plan you confirm, five stages you can see, a review that happens without you, and a revision loop after delivery. Two different answers to the same complaint.

3. Where the check sits

In a notebook, quality control is a social process: someone reads the notebook, notices that a filter dropped a batch of rows, and says so — which works when the someone is careful and has time.

HumanPen Start Project puts a machine check in the path before delivery. The page describes a second AI review and shows what it reports: the primary endpoint matches at 62.2% versus 63.5%, P = 0.072; every analysis population adds up; a clean rerun reproduces the results exactly; all 11 references were found one by one. That is not a substitute for a co-author's judgement, and the page does not claim it is. It is a checklist that runs whether or not anyone has time to run it.

Uploads and what comes back

Deepnote's unit is a project containing a notebook and its environment. The pages we examined describe files and packages living with the kernel, but we found no stated total intake ceiling for a project's data; when a dataset outgrows a laptop, what the page addresses is the machine and the warehouse connection.

HumanPen Start Project states one number for intake: Excel, CSV, Word, PDF and zip up to 5 GB total per project. One ceiling for the whole job rather than a counter per document — the right shape when a survey export, a codebook, a folder of reference PDFs and a journal template are one study, not four uploads.

What comes back differs just as sharply. Deepnote gives you the notebook; whether there is a report at the end depends on whether you write one. HumanPen gives you the report: a .docx with three-line tables and captions, charts at 300 dpi to journal standards, a .pptx with speaker notes, and the code that produced the numbers. The published example is a secondary analysis of the International Stroke Trial: 19,435 patients in the full analysis set, 62.2% of the aspirin group and 63.5% of the control group dead or dependent at 6 months, OR 0.95 (0.89–1.00), P = 0.072, with a PubMed search and every DOI checked, including IST and CAST in the 1997 Lancet papers.

If the thing you owe someone is a manuscript section, a notebook is evidence that you did the work; a report is the work.

Price shape and data governance

Deepnote prices access to an environment: Free includes unlimited Basic machines at 5 GB RAM and 2 vCPU; Team is $39 per editor per month billed yearly, with -20% shown against monthly billing. The meter is the account rather than the output, so cost is predictable before the analysis starts.

HumanPen Start Project prices a project. The pages we read do not publish a price list for this work, so we are not quoting one here. What they do publish is the constraint you plan around: 5 GB total intake, and the scope written into the plan you confirm.

On governance the two hold different things. Deepnote holds an environment and the notebooks in it; the pages we examined do not describe per-project isolation or a retention period, so read its terms rather than assume. HumanPen holds your data and your drafts, and states the position directly: encryption in transit, every project in its own isolated sandbox, no sharing across users, no use for training, deletion at any time, and retention for 6 months after the last activity with a reminder 3 days before expiry.

If your ethics committee asks who could see the patient-level file, the second half of that paragraph is the answer.

When to choose which

Choose Deepnote if you or your team intend to write and iterate on the analysis yourselves: you want a notebook several people can open, a machine sized for the job, and a result you can re-run cell by cell when a reviewer asks. It is also the honest choice when the method is not settled, because exploring in a notebook is cheaper than writing a plan for a question you have not formulated.

Choose HumanPen Start Project if the analysis is not the part you want to spend the week on. You know the question, you have the data, and what has to exist at the end is a report with figures and tables that someone has checked before it reaches you. The plan-first sequence, the second-AI review and the delivered code exist for exactly that handover.

Use both if your workflow has two halves, which most empirical papers do. Prototype in Deepnote to find out whether the model behaves, which variables survive cleaning and what the figure should look like; then take the settled specification, the cleaned dataset and the journal template into HumanPen Start Project, confirm a plan, and let it produce the report and the figure set. The code that arrives with a delivery also goes straight back into a notebook if you want to keep poking at it.

The failure mode to avoid is treating either as the other: a notebook will not write your results section, and a report will not tell you whether your identification strategy is sound.

FAQ

Is Deepnote free? There is a free tier that includes unlimited Basic machines with 5 GB RAM and 2 vCPU. The Team plan is $39 per editor per month billed yearly, and the pricing page shows -20% against the monthly rate.

Does Deepnote write the analysis for me? Deepnote is a notebook you drive; its pages describe AI assistance inside that notebook, but the unit of work is still a project whose cells someone runs and whose outputs someone reads. If you want the analysis written without you writing the code, that is a different category of product.

Can HumanPen Start Project hand me a notebook instead of a report? The deliverables listed on the page are a .docx report, charts at 300 dpi, three-line tables and captions, a .pptx with speaker notes, and the code. A .ipynb notebook is not among them. If the notebook itself is the deliverable, that is Deepnote's job.

How much data can I attach, and how long is it kept? Excel, CSV, Word, PDF and zip up to 5 GB total per project. Analysis runs on Python or R, analyses originally done in SPSS or Stata are redone in Python or R, Wind and CNKI data must be exported first, and GPU training is not supported. Projects are kept 6 months after the last activity, with a reminder 3 days before expiry, and can be deleted at any time.
