Litmaps vs HumanPen Start Project: Mapping the Citation Network vs Running the Analysis

Litmaps is the better tool when the task in front of you is orientation. You have a few papers, you need to see how they connect, which clusters exist and what has appeared since, and a citation-network map answers that faster than a search results page does. On the free tier Litmaps allows up to 20 inputs, 2 Litmaps and 100 articles per Map, with a monthly summary alert; Pro at $10/month lifts the input, article and Litmap limits and makes alerts configurable.

HumanPen Start Project is the better tool when the task is execution. You have data, a question and a deadline, and what you need at the end is a report with tables, figures and code that someone other than the person who ran it has checked. It plans first, starts only when you confirm, runs Python or R in an isolated sandbox, and delivers a .docx report, 300 dpi figures, three-line tables, slides with speaker notes and the analysis code.

Put briefly: Litmaps draws the shape of a field; HumanPen finishes the study that the field suggested.

How the workflows differ

1. One starts from a paper, the other from a question

Litmaps begins with an input: you name a paper and the product grows a map around it. The whole vocabulary of the pricing page is article-shaped — inputs, articles per Map, Litmaps. That is a good unit for a literature review in its early phase, because the thing you are trying to establish is relational: what sits next to what, which line of work a paper belongs to, and where the map goes thin.

HumanPen Start Project begins with a sentence: what you need done, and what you have attached. The unit of work is a project, and the files can be a cleaned CSV, a codebook, a folder of PDFs, a template and a zip. Nothing about that intake assumes the input is an article; it assumes the input is evidence you intend to analyse.

The consequence lands on the second step. After Litmaps has shown you the shape of a field, you still have to decide what to measure, run it and write it up. After HumanPen has delivered, you have a report and a figure set, but you still have to judge whether the field you mapped is the right one.

2. Reading a map vs approving a plan

Litmaps is read-first. You look at a picture, zoom, click through, and the artefact is your own reading of the field. There is nothing to configure beyond your inputs and your alert schedule.

HumanPen Start Project is approve-first. The page describes five stages — Describe, Plan, In progress, In review, Delivered — and work starts when you confirm the plan. If the request is underspecified, the project asks; the public example shows the system restating the requirement before doing anything: the full analysis set is 19,435 patients, the primary endpoint is death or dependency at 6 months, and about 150 missing outcomes get a sensitivity analysis. That restatement is the plan.

That difference matters when the cost of a wrong turn is asymmetric. Browsing a map and finding it unhelpful costs ten minutes. Running the wrong model on a two-year panel and writing it up costs a week.

3. Staying current vs closing a round

Litmaps has a monitoring loop that HumanPen does not: alerts. On the free tier they arrive as a monthly summary; Pro makes them weekly or configurable. If your problem is that the literature moves while your review sits in a drawer, that feature solves it, and no amount of analysis capability substitutes for it.

HumanPen's recurring loop is internal to a delivered project. Request a revision inside the project; the scope is stated first, work starts after you confirm, a new version is delivered and the previous versions are kept. That is a different kind of iteration — not "what is new in the field" but "what is wrong with this table" — and it is the loop that consumes the last two weeks of a paper.

Uploading files and what comes back

The two products take different things in and give different things back, and that is where most of the practical difference sits.

Litmaps takes articles. The pricing page counts what you give it in inputs and articles per Map — 20 inputs, 2 Litmaps and 100 articles per Map on the free tier, no ceiling on Pro. What comes back is a map you can read and share, plus alerts. The pages we examined do not describe a file upload with a size limit, a statistical run, or a bundle of deliverables.

HumanPen Start Project takes a mixed bundle: Excel, CSV, Word, PDF and zip up to 5 GB total. That ceiling is stated once, for the whole project, rather than per document — the right shape when the unit is a study, since a survey export, a codebook, twenty reference PDFs and a journal template are one project, not four uploads against four counters.

What comes back is specified on the page: a .docx report, charts at 300 dpi to journal standards, three-line tables and captions, a .pptx with speaker notes, and the code. The public example is a secondary analysis of the International Stroke Trial: 19,435 patients in the full analysis set, 62.2% of the aspirin group and 63.5% of the control group dead or dependent at 6 months, OR 0.95 (0.89–1.00), P = 0.072, with a PubMed search and every DOI checked. Before delivery a second AI reviews the output; the page shows the result as "Review passed (round 1)", with the endpoint matching, the analysis populations adding up, a clean rerun reproducing the numbers and all 11 references found one by one.

That is a different category of output from a map: if the deliverable is a figure a reviewer will interrogate, a map will not get you there; if it is a picture of a field, a report will not either.

Price shape and data governance

Litmaps prices access. Free covers basic search with the input, map and article limits and a monthly summary alert; Pro at $10/month removes those limits and lets you configure alerts; a Team tier extends unlimited maps and collaboration across a team. The meter is the account rather than the output, so cost is predictable at the start of a review and detached from how much work the review turns out to be.

HumanPen Start Project prices a project. There is no per-page meter, and the constraint the product asks you to plan around is capacity — 5 GB total — plus the scope in the plan you confirm. The HumanPen pages we read do not publish a price list for this work, so we are not quoting one.

Governance is where the two are least comparable, because they hold different things. Litmaps holds references and map state; its pages we examined do not describe per-project isolation or a retention period, so read its terms rather than assume. HumanPen holds your data and drafts, and states the position plainly: transport encryption, every project in its own isolated sandbox, no sharing across users, no use for training, deletion at any time, and retention for 6 months after the last activity with a reminder 3 days before expiry.

If your ethics committee asks where the analysis ran and who could see the patient-level file, that last paragraph answers it. If your question is where the reading list lives, Litmaps answers it, and it is a lighter question.

When to choose which

Choose Litmaps if the immediate job is to understand a body of work: you are starting a review, you want clusters and gaps, you want new papers to reach you without searching again, and you want to choose which paper to read next from a picture rather than a list. It takes very little setup.

Choose HumanPen Start Project if the immediate job is to produce something: you have data and a method, the output has to be a report with figures and tables, and the number in it has to survive a co-author asking how it was computed. The plan-first sequence, the second-AI review and the delivered code exist for that situation.

Use both if your workflow is two-stage, which for most empirical papers it is. Map the field in Litmaps to fix the framing and keep references current; then take the dataset, the method and the gap into HumanPen Start Project, confirm a plan, and let it run the analysis and produce the draft. The handoff is a sentence and a folder of files.

The failure mode to avoid is using either one for the other's job — asking a map to produce a regression, or asking a report generator to tell you which papers you have missed.

FAQ

Is Litmaps free? There is a free tier: basic search, up to 20 inputs, 2 Litmaps, 100 articles per Map and a monthly summary alert. Pro at $10/month removes the input, article and Litmap limits and makes alerts configurable, and a Team tier extends unlimited maps and collaboration across a team.

Does Litmaps run statistical analysis on my data? The public pages we examined describe discovery, citation-network visualisation and literature alerts. They do not describe a statistical execution environment, an upload with a size limit, or a code deliverable. If your next step is a regression on a dataset rather than a map of papers, that step belongs in a different tool.

Can HumanPen Start Project draw a citation network for me? No — it is not a citation-mapping product and its pages do not describe one. What it does with literature is narrower and more concrete: it reads your papers, searches for more, builds an evidence table and writes a review, with the DOI of each reference checked individually.

How much data can I attach to a HumanPen Start Project? Excel, CSV, Word, PDF and zip up to 5 GB total per project. Analysis runs on Python or R, analyses originally done in SPSS or Stata are redone in Python or R, Wind and CNKI data must be exported first, and GPU training is not supported.
