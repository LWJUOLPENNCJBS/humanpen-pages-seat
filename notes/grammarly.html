Grammarly vs HumanPen Start Project: Editing Every Sentence vs Producing the Study

Grammarly is the better tool when the text already exists and the problem is how it reads. It is a general writing assistant, and the pages we examined present it that way: mistake-free writing, tone, clarity, and rewriting at the level of the sentence. Free includes 100 AI prompts and tone visibility; Pro is $12 per member per month billed annually — $30 when billed monthly — with 2,000 AI prompts, full-sentence rewrites and brand consistency; Enterprise is contact sales, with unlimited members and confidential mode.

HumanPen Start Project is the better tool when the text does not exist yet because the analysis does not exist yet. You attach the data — Excel, CSV, Word, PDF or zip, up to 5 GB total — describe what the study needs, confirm a plan, and the work runs in Python or R. What arrives is a .docx report with three-line tables and captions, figures at 300 dpi, a .pptx with speaker notes, and the code, after a second AI has reviewed it.

The distinction is worth stating plainly because the two products are often put in the same shopping list. Grammarly improves prose wherever you already type. HumanPen produces the numbers the prose has to be about, and then the document around them.

How the workflows differ

1. A layer over your text versus a container for your data

Grammarly assumes the document exists. The whole product is shaped around that assumption: it sits where you type, watches what you write, and offers changes. The pricing page sells prompts and rewrites rather than projects or datasets, and the marketing copy talks about how writing lands on a reader — tone, confidence, clarity, brand.

HumanPen Start Project assumes the document does not exist. You cannot start by writing, because there is nothing to write about until the analysis runs. The intake is a description plus files, and the first response is a plan: five stages from Describe to Delivered, work starting only on your confirmation. In the published example the plan restates the job before executing it — full analysis set of 19,435 patients, primary endpoint death or dependency at 6 months, roughly 150 missing outcomes handled in a sensitivity analysis.

That difference decides which tool is useful on which day: when the draft exists, Grammarly has work to do; when the dataset arrives, it has nothing to edit yet.

2. What "quality" is being measured

Grammarly's quality signal is reader-facing: does this sentence read clearly, does the tone fit, does it match the brand. Free shows you your tone and gives 100 AI prompts; Pro rewrites full sentences, adjusts tone, keeps you on-brand and raises the allowance to 2,000 AI prompts. For a grant abstract, a cover letter or an email to a collaborator, that is the right axis, and it is why the product is used far outside academia.

HumanPen's quality signal is internal: is the number in the report the number the analysis produced. The page shows the review step reporting "Review passed (round 1)" with the endpoint matching at 62.2% versus 63.5%, P = 0.072, every analysis population adding up, a clean rerun reproducing the results exactly, and all 11 references found one by one. It also describes a rewrite pass that removes stock phrasing while keeping numbers and citations exactly as they are.

Those two signals are not rivals: a sentence can be perfectly clear and describe the wrong odds ratio, and a table can be reproducible and unreadable.

3. Where the tool stops

Grammarly stops at the edge of the text. That is a feature, not a limitation: it does not want your dataset, it wants your paragraph. The pages we examined do not describe statistical execution, a data sandbox or a code deliverable.

HumanPen stops at the edge of the project. It takes the dataset precisely because the deliverable is downstream of it, and it publishes the constraints that come with that: 5 GB total intake, Python or R rather than SPSS or Stata directly, Wind and CNKI data exported first, no GPU training.

The useful question is not which one is more capable. It is which edge your problem sits inside.

Uploads and what comes back

Grammarly's input is text you have already written, pasted or typed into whatever surface you use. There is no intake ceiling to plan around because there is no dataset to upload; the unit is the sentence, which is why it fits a literature review, a response to reviewers or a methods paragraph you have rewritten four times.

HumanPen Start Project's input is a bundle: Excel, CSV, Word, PDF and zip up to 5 GB total per project. One ceiling for the whole job, which is the right shape when the job is a study — a survey export, a codebook, a folder of reference PDFs and a journal template arrive together.

What comes back is where the two are least comparable. Grammarly returns edited text: the same document, clearer. HumanPen returns a package — the .docx report with three-line tables and captions, charts at 300 dpi to journal standards, a .pptx with speaker notes, and the code. The published example is a secondary analysis of the International Stroke Trial: 19,435 patients in the full analysis set, 62.2% of the aspirin group and 63.5% of the control group dead or dependent at 6 months, OR 0.95 (0.89–1.00), P = 0.072, with a PubMed search and every DOI checked, including IST and CAST in the 1997 Lancet papers.

If you already have those numbers you need the first kind of output; if you do not, no amount of editing will produce them.

Price shape and data governance

Grammarly prices seats and volume. Free is $0 with 100 AI prompts and tone visibility. Pro is $12 per member per month billed annually, or $30 when billed monthly, with 2,000 AI prompts, full-sentence rewrites, tone adjustment and brand consistency. Enterprise is quoted through sales, and the tier lists unlimited members, confidential mode, granular roles and permissions, and data loss prevention. The meter is people and volume, which maps onto a team that knows its headcount.

HumanPen Start Project prices a project, and the pages we read do not publish a price list for that work, so we are not quoting one. What they do publish is what you plan around: 5 GB total intake and the scope recorded in the plan you confirmed.

The two hold different things. Grammarly's home page sets out its position on user content; read that with its terms for a data question about prose. The pages we examined do not describe per-project isolation or a retention period for uploaded files. HumanPen holds datasets and drafts, and states the position directly: encryption in transit, every project in its own isolated sandbox, no sharing across users, no use for training, deletion at any time, and retention for 6 months after the last activity with a reminder 3 days before expiry.

For an ethics application, the sentence that matters is the one about the isolated sandbox and the training data. For a style guide rollout, the sentence that matters is on Grammarly's page, not this one.

When to choose which

Choose Grammarly if the manuscript exists and the job is to make it read better, consistently, across a team. It is the right tool for the last mile, and deliberately not specialised to academic writing — an advantage when the same week contains a grant abstract, a press release and forty emails.

Choose HumanPen Start Project if the document cannot be written yet. You have the data and the question, the output has to contain a table a reviewer will check, and someone other than you should have verified the numbers before you see them. The plan-first sequence and the second-AI review exist for that situation.

Use both if your paper has the usual two halves, which nearly all of them do. Run the analysis through HumanPen Start Project, take the delivered .docx and figures, and put the prose through Grammarly before it goes to co-authors. The two touch different parts of the file and do not conflict: one fixes whether the number is right, the other fixes whether the sentence carrying it is readable.

The mistake to avoid is buying one for the other's job: an editing layer will not compute a hazard ratio, and a report still benefits from something that cares about commas.

FAQ

Is Grammarly free? There is a $0 tier with 100 AI prompts and tone visibility, aimed at mistake-free writing. Pro is $12 per member per month billed annually, or $30 when billed monthly, and adds full-sentence rewrites, tone adjustment, brand consistency and 2,000 AI prompts. Enterprise is priced through sales and includes unlimited members and confidential mode.

Can Grammarly analyse my research data? The pages we examined describe writing assistance: proofreading, clarity, tone, rewrites, brand consistency. They do not describe a statistical execution environment, a Python or R sandbox, or a code deliverable. If the task is a regression on a dataset, it is a different category of tool.

Does Grammarly handle citations for a journal submission? Citation consistency is listed among the things Pro can check, which is a real help in a long document. The pages we examined do not describe a reference library, a citation-style catalogue or a bibliography export, so plan for those separately — as with most general writing tools.

Is HumanPen Start Project a replacement for Grammarly? No. Its deliverables are a report, figures, tables, slides and code, produced in a project after you confirm a plan; the pages do not describe an editing layer inside your word processor. It replaces the analysis and the write-up, not the final read-through.
