Jenni AI vs HumanPen Start Project: Autocomplete in Your Draft vs a Delivered Analysis

Jenni is the better tool when the sentence is the unit of work. You are in a document, you know roughly what you want to say, and an autocomplete that continues your paragraph and drops a citation in behind the claim is exactly the help you need. On the Free plan that is 10 AI autocompletes per day, 5 AI requests, 10 PDF uploads with a 25 MB / 150-page ceiling per file; Plus at $12/month raises it to 5,000 autocompletes per month, 500 AI requests, unlimited PDF uploads and 100 MB / 500 pages; Pro at $29/month makes autocomplete, requests and reviews unlimited and allows PDFs up to 1,000 pages.

HumanPen Start Project is the better tool when the number is the unit of work. You hand over a dataset and a question, confirm a plan, and what comes back is a report: a .docx with three-line tables and captions, figures at 300 dpi, a .pptx with speaker notes, and the code that produced every figure. The prose is written around numbers that came out of your data, not around a sentence you were halfway through.

These are not competing answers to one question. Jenni helps you produce text; HumanPen produces the analysis and the document that reports it. If what you need this week is a discussion section that finally reads well, Jenni is pointed at the right target. If what you need is a table of baseline characteristics and the paragraph that explains it, that is a different job.

How the workflows differ

1. The cursor versus the plan

Jenni meets you where the work already is: in the document. The company describes an AI workspace where researchers read, write and cite, with every claim traceable to the source, and the pricing page sells autocompletes and requests rather than projects. The centre of gravity is the drafting surface: you type, it continues, you accept or reject, and the paragraph grows. The free plan's 10 autocompletes a day is enough to feel that before committing.

HumanPen Start Project has no cursor to sit behind. You arrive with files and a request, and the first thing it does is restate the job: the whole analysis set is 19,435 patients, the endpoint is death or dependency at 6 months, about 150 missing outcomes get a sensitivity analysis. That restatement is the plan, and work starts only when you confirm it. Five stages — Describe, Plan, In progress, In review, Delivered — tell you where you are.

2. Citations as formatting versus citations as retrieval

Jenni's citation machinery is broad: 10,000+ citation styles and library export in .ris, .bib and .csv, so whatever reference manager or journal you target, the handoff exists. For a draft moving between Zotero, a Word file and a submission portal, that removes real friction.

HumanPen's citation work is narrower and heavier. The published example shows a PubMed search on aspirin in acute ischaemic stroke, a returned set including the International Stroke Trial and CAST from the 1997 Lancet papers, and every DOI checked; the review step reports "All 11 references found, one by one". Numbers and citations are kept exactly as they are.

Jenni gets the citation into the right style; HumanPen checks that the reference exists and says what the sentence claims.

3. What the review covers

Jenni sells Reviews as a document-level pass that scans the whole document to flag improvements in citations and writing quality: a useful second reader for prose.

HumanPen's review asks whether the analysis survives a rerun. The page shows the reviewer confirming that the primary endpoint matches — 62.2% in the aspirin group against 63.5% in the control group, OR 0.95 (0.89–1.00), P = 0.072 — that every analysis population adds up, that a clean rerun reproduces the results exactly, and that the references are accounted for. Then it delivers.

One review improves how the paper reads; the other improves whether it is right. A manuscript needs both.

Uploads and what comes back

Jenni takes PDFs, and the ceiling is per file and per plan: 25 MB and 150 pages on Free, 100 MB and 500 pages on Plus, 1,000 pages on Pro, with 10 PDF uploads on Free. Right for a stack of papers you intend to cite; not for a multi-hundred-megabyte survey export.

HumanPen Start Project takes one mixed bundle and states one ceiling: Excel, CSV, Word, PDF and zip up to 5 GB total per project. One number for the whole job, because the job is a study — the dataset, the codebook, the reference PDFs and the journal template are one intake.

The outputs diverge. Jenni returns text into your document, with citations attached. HumanPen returns a package: a .docx report with three-line tables and captions, charts at 300 dpi to journal standards, a .pptx with speaker notes for the group meeting, and the code. The published example is a full secondary analysis of the International Stroke Trial with 19,435 patients in the full analysis set, 62.2% versus 63.5% dead or dependent at 6 months, and the sensitivity analysis on the roughly 150 missing outcomes.

If your reader is a supervisor marking prose, the first output is what they asked for. If your reader is a reviewer asking how the odds ratio was computed, only the second one answers.

Price shape and data governance

Jenni prices allowance. Free is $0 with 10 autocompletes a day, 5 AI requests, 10 PDF uploads and a 25 MB / 150-page limit per PDF. Plus is $12/month for 5,000 autocompletes, 500 AI requests, unlimited PDF uploads and 100 MB / 500 pages. Pro is $29/month with unlimited autocomplete, requests and reviews and PDFs to 1,000 pages. The meter tracks drafting volume, so a light month costs the same as a heavy one within a tier.

HumanPen Start Project prices a project. The pages we read do not publish a price list for this work, so we are not quoting one. What they do publish is the boundary you plan against: 5 GB total intake and the scope in the plan you confirmed.

Governance is short because the two hold different things. Jenni holds your drafts, your library and your PDFs; the pages we examined do not describe per-project isolation or a retention period, so read its terms rather than assume. HumanPen holds your data and your drafts and states the position in plain terms: encrypted in transit, each project in its own isolated sandbox, never shared across users or used for training, deletable at any time, kept 6 months after the last activity with a reminder 3 days before expiry.

If the question is whether your unpublished dataset can leave your laptop, the second paragraph answers it; if it is where your reading list lives, the first one does.

When to choose which

Choose Jenni if the work in front of you is writing you steer sentence by sentence: a discussion section that needs to be longer, an introduction that needs citations in the right style, a draft that has to sound like you. The autocomplete and the 10,000+ citation styles are pointed precisely at that; the page puts its own audience at eight million writers.

Choose HumanPen Start Project if the writing is downstream of analysis you have not run. You have the data, the endpoint and the target journal, and what has to exist is a report whose numbers can be reproduced. The plan-first sequence, the second-AI review and the delivered code are there because the hard part is not the prose.

Use both if your paper has the usual two halves. Take the reading and the introduction to Jenni, where a sentence at a time is the right increment; take the dataset, the method and the results to HumanPen Start Project, confirm a plan, and get the report, figures and code back. If the discussion then needs tightening, that is editing work again — and editing is what a drafting tool is for.

The mistake to avoid is expecting either to do the other's job. An autocomplete will not produce a Table 1 that reconciles with your analysis set, and a delivered report will not sound like you wrote it at 1 a.m., which for some sections is exactly what you want.

FAQ

Is Jenni free? There is a $0 plan with 10 AI autocompletes per day, 5 AI requests, 10 PDF uploads and a 25 MB / 150-page limit per PDF. Plus is $12/month for 5,000 autocompletes, 500 AI requests, unlimited PDF uploads and 100 MB / 500 pages; Pro is $29/month with unlimited autocomplete, requests and reviews.

Can Jenni run the statistical analysis on my dataset? The pages we examined describe a writing and citation workspace with a PDF library and document-level reviews. They do not describe a statistical execution environment, Python or R, or a code deliverable. If your next step is a regression rather than a paragraph, that step belongs somewhere else.

Will HumanPen Start Project write my whole paper for me? It produces a report or a draft as a project deliverable — .docx with tables and captions, figures, slides with speaker notes, and the code — with the literature searched and every DOI checked. It is not an autocomplete inside your word processor. What it does well is the part where a number has to be defensible.

How much can I upload, and are my files used for training? Excel, CSV, Word, PDF and zip up to 5 GB total per project. Each project runs in its own isolated sandbox, data is not shared across users and not used for training, and it can be deleted at any time. Projects are kept 6 months after the last activity with a reminder 3 days before expiry.
