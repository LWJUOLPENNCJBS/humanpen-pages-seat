An undergraduate thesis in eight weeks: a realistic plan with HumanPen Start Project

An undergraduate thesis is a small project with a hard deadline, a supervisor who has forty other students, and a committee that will read the methods section closely. The usual failure is not ambition. It is discovering in week six that the survey data cannot answer the question, and having no time to change either.

This is the eight-week plan we would give a student who has a topic, a dataset or the ability to collect one, and HumanPen Start Project available for the analysis and the document.

Week one: choose a question you can actually answer

The most common undergraduate thesis failure is a question whose data do not exist. Before writing anything, name the dataset, the number of observations you expect, and the variable that measures the outcome. If any of the three is uncertain, spend this week resolving it rather than writing an introduction.

Write the question as a comparison: does X differ between groups, is Y associated with Z after adjusting for W. A topic is not a question, and a question without a comparison cannot produce a result.

Week two: build the codebook before the analysis

Document every variable: name, label, unit, coding, missing-value rule. Where a variable is derived, write the derivation. This document is what makes week four possible, and it is also the appendix that committees ask for.

If you are collecting data, collect less and document more. A short survey with a clean codebook produces a defensible thesis; a long survey with ambiguous items produces a methods section that cannot be written.

Week three: descriptives first, and agree the denominators

Produce the counts, the distributions and the missingness table before any modelling. Show them to your supervisor. Most disagreements about results are actually disagreements about who is in the sample, and they are much cheaper to settle at this stage.

This is also where a data problem surfaces while there is still time to fix it: an implausible value, a category with four cases, a variable that is constant within a group.

Week four: specify the analysis before running it

Write the analysis as a plan: the population, the outcome, the exposure or comparison, the model, and the table you expect. Then run it.

HumanPen Start Project asks for requirements, proposes a plan and starts only after you approve it, which fits an undergraduate timeline well because the approval step is a natural checkpoint with your supervisor. Read the plan and check three things: the population, the outcome definition, and whether the deliverable is what your department requires.

For the analysis itself, attach the cleaned file and the codebook; Excel, CSV, Word, PDF and zip are accepted up to 5 GB total, and the analysis runs in Python or R, so the code you receive is inspectable when your committee asks how a number was produced.

Week five: figures and results, with the interpretation switched off

Write the results section as a list of sentences that each point at a table or a figure. No adjectives, no mechanism, no literature. This is the section that a committee reads most closely and the one that is easiest to get right.

Figures arrive at 300 dpi with three-line tables and captions, which is above what an undergraduate thesis needs and exactly what is required if the work is later submitted to a journal. Decide which figure carries the argument and put the rest in an appendix.

Week six: methods that a reader could repeat

Write the methods from the plan and the codebook: population, variables, model, software, missing-data handling. Specificity is the whole requirement. "Data were analysed using standard statistical methods" tells a reader nothing and invites the question you least want.

If prior work was done in SPSS or Stata, say that the delivered analysis was re-done in Python or R, and name the language. That sentence is both accurate and useful to the next student.

Week seven: discussion bounded by the design

The discussion answers three questions: what the result shows, what it does not show, and what would be needed to show more. An undergraduate thesis is not expected to change a field; it is expected to be honest about its scope.

Check the verbs. A cross-sectional association is not a cause. A non-significant difference is not proof of no difference. A subgroup pattern is a hypothesis.

Week eight: format, check, submit

Departmental formatting, page limits, reference style, declaration forms. Then the four mechanical checks: the numbers in the abstract match the tables, the outcome is defined once, every reference resolves, and every figure is at a printable resolution.

Those four are the same integrity checks HumanPen Start Project runs before delivery — the primary endpoint matches, populations add up, a clean rerun reproduces the results, and references are found one by one — and they are worth running on the assembled document even though you did not assemble it alone.

When the plan slips

If week four arrives and the data cannot answer the question, narrow the question rather than extending the deadline. A smaller, well-answered question is a pass; a large, unanswered one is a resubmission. Tell your supervisor at week four, not week seven.

Schedule the checkpoint your supervisor will actually attend

Supervisors miss meetings; they rarely miss a document. Send something written before each meeting: the question in week one, the codebook in week two, the descriptive table in week three, the analysis plan in week four. A written artefact gets a written response, and a written response is what you can act on.

If a meeting is cancelled, do not wait. Send the artefact with two specific questions, and continue with the week's work. Eight weeks does not contain much slack.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need statistics software installed?

No. Upload the data and the codebook, approve the plan, and download the report, tables, figures and code. The analysis runs in Python or R on the service side, and you keep the code for your appendix.

Will my supervisor accept a report produced this way?

That depends on your department's rules on assistance, which you should read in week one. The substantive requirement is that you understand and can defend every number, which is why the code and the plan are part of the deliverable.

How much does it cost?

Credit use depends on the scope you confirm; the product bills by actual usage and a failed task is not charged, and the plan is confirmed before work starts. Check the current pricing page for the packages rather than relying on a figure from a blog post, including this one.

What if I need to change the analysis after seeing the results?

Request a revision in the project: the scope is explained first, work starts after you confirm, and a new version is delivered while the earlier version is kept. Report both analyses honestly if the change was prompted by the data.
