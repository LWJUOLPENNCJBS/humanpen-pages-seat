Reverso vs HumanPen Translate: A Translator With Reference Tools vs a Document Pipeline

Short answer

Choose Reverso when you are working with language rather than with a file: a word you half-remember, a phrase you want to see in a real sentence, a clause whose grammar you doubt, a passage you need in 28 languages. Its public page describes free translation, a dictionary, grammar check and context examples. It also translates documents — Word, PDF, PowerPoint and Excel — "...in 25+ languages", "keeping their layout".

Choose HumanPen Translate when the deliverable is the file. It accepts PDF, Word (docx), PowerPoint (pptx), Excel (xlsx), EPUB, HTML and plain text and returns the format you sent: charts, formulas, hyperlinks and page structure parsed, rich-text styling preserved. It covers 12 languages at 10 credits per 1,000 words, spent from the same balance as the humanize, condense and citation tools.

This pair overlaps more than most, and the overlap is real — both pages discuss documents, and both make a claim about layout. What differs is what each workspace is for. Reverso's page describes a translator plus dictionary plus grammar tool; it does not describe a single workspace that also humanizes, condenses and fixes citations on the same credit balance. That is a difference in neighbours, not in craft.

Where the two tools actually diverge

1. "Keeping their layout" and "preserved styling" are not the same sentence

Both pages promise something about layout, which is why this comparison needs more care than the others. Reverso's wording is about a translated document that still looks like the original: "keeping their layout", applied to Word, PDF, PowerPoint and Excel files. That is a claim about appearance — and for a copy a colleague will read, appearance is the right thing to promise.

HumanPen Translate's page names the objects instead: charts, formulas, hyperlinks and page structure are parsed, rich-text styling is preserved, and the file returns in the format you sent. Naming formulas and charts is a heavier commitment than naming layout, because those objects fail quietly. If a formula collapses into its computed number, nothing looks wrong; the file has simply stopped being a working file.

The practical test is what happens after the translation lands. If the answer is "someone reads it", Reverso's stated claim covers the case. If it is "someone edits it, and next month it goes to a printer or a journal", the question becomes whether the formulas are still formulas and the charts still charts — and only one of the two answers that.

2. Twenty-eight and twenty-five-plus against twelve

Here the numbers favour Reverso. Its page states "Translate text 28 languages" and describes documents translated "...in 25+ languages". HumanPen Translate names 12: Chinese (Simplified and Traditional), English, Japanese, Korean, Spanish, French, Portuguese, Russian, German, Polish and Italian.

The units differ slightly — Reverso's 28 is for text, its 25+ for documents; HumanPen's 12 is one set applied to every format — but the direction is not ambiguous. If your pair falls outside the twelve, no amount of document fidelity compensates. What HumanPen is trading for that narrower list is the commitment described above: the file comes back with its structure intact.

The language count is not a scoreboard. It is a filter you apply first, before any of the document questions become relevant.

3. What sits next to the translate button

The most interesting difference is not in translation at all. It is the shelf of tools beside it.

Reverso's neighbours are reference tools for someone working with language: a dictionary, grammar check, context examples showing a phrase inside real sentences, and — in Premium — unlimited AI correction and rephrase. That is a translator's desk.

HumanPen's neighbours are document operations: humanize, condense and citation fix, all drawing on the same credit balance that Translate spends at 10 credits per 1,000 words. That is closer to a production line for one document: translate it, tighten it to a word budget, convert its citations, adjust its voice, and pay for each step from a balance that never expires.

Neither shelf is fuller. They are furnished for different rooms.

When to choose which (or use both)

Choose Reverso if your work is word- and phrase-level, if you want a dictionary and real usage examples beside the translation, if your pair is outside the twelve languages HumanPen Translate names, or if you need a translated copy that looks like the original and will be read rather than edited. Its page also states document translation across Word, PDF, PowerPoint and Excel in 25+ languages.

Choose HumanPen Translate if the file returns to an editing workflow: a manuscript that keeps its heading numbering, a deck whose charts carry live text, a workbook whose formulas must survive. It also fits when you need to condense or restyle the same document afterwards, since those operations share one balance.

Use both. Look up terminology in Reverso while preparing a document, then run the finished file through HumanPen Translate when it has to be delivered. If your pair is outside the twelve, invert the order: translate with Reverso, and keep HumanPen for the other operations on your original-language master.

FAQ

Q. Does Reverso keep the layout of a translated document? A. Yes, as stated: its page describes translating Word, PDF, PowerPoint and Excel documents "...in 25+ languages", "keeping their layout". What it does not describe is whether formulas and charts come back as editable objects — a different claim, and one only the HumanPen Translate page makes.

Q. Does HumanPen Translate replace a dictionary? A. No. Its page describes a document translation operation, not a dictionary, grammar checker, or set of usage examples. Those belong on the Reverso side of this comparison.

Q. Do the two cover the same languages? A. No. Reverso states "Translate text 28 languages" and documents in 25+ languages; HumanPen Translate names 12. If your pair is outside those twelve, Reverso is the only one that can translate it at all.

Q. How is HumanPen Translate priced? A. 1,000 words costs 10 credits. Credits never expire, work in the web app and the API, and come from the same balance as the humanize, condense and citation tools. New accounts get 100 free credits, and failed or cancelled document jobs are never charged.

Q. What happens to my file afterwards? A. Job files are kept 7 days and then removed; the job record stays. Data is encrypted in transit, never used for training, and deletable.
